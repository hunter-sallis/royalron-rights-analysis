A second work, MV0VBP, lists only you and has Create Songs Publishing claiming the same 11.66%. The same recording is also matched to MV5M3M (Jay Rich’s share, filed separately by The Mezzo Agency) and to MS2T0P, an unrelated Colombian song called “My Love” by Andres Felipe Rodriguez, claimed at 100% through SAYCO / Klaim Music. Claims on this one recording add up to about 185%. A recording can only carry 100% of a composition, so the duplicates have to be reconciled into the one work that lists every writer before any of these shares can be relied on. The Colombian link is a title-collision mismatch: that song has nothing to do with this recording, and the fix is a dispute of the link rather than a negotiation.

Which one depends on your agreements — if Create Songs Publishing holds your publishing through a distribution or admin deal, Royal Ron Pub should not also be filing, and the reverse.

What happens next

  1. Put your identity on one record

    Ask The MLC to consolidate your four writer records under IPI 01066519647. Before that, tell us which society you belong to and whether RON K ROYAL (IPI 00183199934) is you — if it is, your society needs to confirm which of the two numbers is the right one. Send us your full placement list too; Genius shows four songs, and your catalog is clearly bigger than that.

    The MLC — member portalBMI Songview — check your registrationsASCAP ACE — check your registrations

  2. Choose one publisher per song

    Decide, from your agreements, whether Royal Ron Pub or Create Songs Publishing represents you on each song. Then the duplicate on My Love (MV0VBP) and on As Much (AW4PEE) can be retired in favour of the work that carries the recording and every writer.

  3. Clean up My Love

    Merge the duplicate registrations into the main work, MV3JF2, which already lists all seven writers. Separately, dispute the link between the EST Gee recording and the unrelated Colombian “My Love” (MS2T0P, Klaim Music via SAYCO). Jay Rich’s separate filing (MV5M3M, The Mezzo Agency) is part of the same merge.

  4. Get onto the works that carry your recordings

    For Keep Going, What If I Told You and Haunted House, the recording sits on a work that lists only the artist’s side. For each one we need whatever exists in writing about the split — a split sheet, a producer agreement, a beat licence, or a text or DM where it was agreed. The people whose paperwork unlocks these: YN Jay (Jaylein Cantrell, via Create Digital Music), Millyz (Myles Lockwood, via Empire Strikes First) and Loomin, and Baby Simba (Curtis Blocker, via Songtrust). Then add a publisher for you on Icebox and Back in Memphis, and confirm Pull the Alarm is yours. We will never file a claim on your behalf without documentation.

  5. File every new placement complete

    One work per song, every writer on it with their IPI, one publisher per writer, and the ISRC attached at the time of filing. That single habit prevents every category of problem in this report from recurring. When you are ready, the master side is the next review: on My Love, a Letter of Direction from the artist to SoundExchange is how a producer’s share of digital performance royalties is paid.
